A café POS used to mean a billing machine at the counter. Today it is the system your whole café runs on: how guests order, how the kitchen hears about it, how bills are printed, how money is counted at night and how you know whether the month made a profit.

There is no single “best” POS for every café. A 12-table café in Hauz Khas, a cloud kitchen in Gurugram and a 60-cover family restaurant need different things. What you can do is choose the right one for **how your café actually runs**. This checklist is built for that.

> **The short answer:** pick software that prints correct GST bills, takes UPI, cash and card on the same bill, sends orders to the kitchen without paper getting lost, keeps billing when the internet drops, and runs on the phones or tablets you already own. Then make the vendor show you each of those with your own menu.

## Start with how your café runs

Before you look at any feature list, write down three things:

1. **Where your orders come from.** Mostly dine-in? Counter and takeaway? Mostly delivery apps? A dine-in café gains the most from table ordering and a kitchen screen. A delivery-heavy kitchen needs strong delivery-app integration first.
2. **How service works.** Do guests order at the counter, does a waiter take orders at the table, or would you like guests to order from their own phones?
3. **Who uses it.** If your staff change often, the system has to be learnable in one shift. If you are the owner and the cashier, it has to be fast with one hand.

Keep this note next to you during every demo. A long feature list means nothing if the basics of your own day are slow.

## The must-haves for an Indian café

These are not nice-to-haves. If a POS gets any of them wrong, you will feel it every single day.

| Must-have | Why it matters | What to check in the demo |
|---|---|---|
| Correct GST tax invoices | Wrong bills mean trouble with your CA and with customers who need an invoice | GSTIN and FSSAI number on the bill, CGST and SGST shown separately, a clean invoice series for each financial year |
| UPI, cash and card on one bill | Indian tables often split a bill | Can one bill be paid partly by UPI and partly by cash, and does the day’s closing show each? |
| Orders reach the kitchen reliably | Lost paper slips are the most common rush-hour mistake | A kitchen screen or a printed KOT for every order, with table, quantity and notes |
| Works when the internet drops | Wi-Fi fails exactly when the café is full | Turn Wi-Fi off during the demo and try to make a bill |
| Runs on your devices | New hardware is expensive and locks you in | Does it run in a browser on an Android tablet, an iPad and a laptop? |
| Easy menu editing | Prices, sizes and add-ons change often | Add a dish with two sizes and an extra-shot add-on in under a minute |
| Reports you will actually read | You need to know sales, payment split and best sellers without exporting spreadsheets | Today, yesterday and this month, by payment type and by dish |

## Features that pay for themselves

Once the basics are right, a few features change the economics of a small café.

- **QR table ordering.** Guests scan a card on the table and order from their own phone, without downloading an app. At rush hour, your staff stop running between tables to take orders and start serving. Read our [QR ordering guide](/blog/qr-code-ordering-for-restaurants/) before you switch it on.
- **A kitchen screen.** Orders appear on a tablet in the kitchen with how long each has waited, and staff are told the moment food is ready.
- **A guest list.** If guests can leave their name and number (with permission), you can bring regulars back on slow days instead of paying for ads. See [how to get repeat customers](/blog/how-to-get-repeat-customers-cafe/).
- **Purchases and profit.** When you log what you spend on stock, the POS can show profit, not just sales. Many cafés discover that a “good month” was not one.

## Questions to ask in every demo

Ask these out loud, and ask to see the answer on screen rather than hear it:

1. “Can you set up two of my real dishes, with sizes and add-ons, right now?”
2. “Show me a split payment: ₹500 UPI and the rest in cash.”
3. “Turn off the Wi-Fi. Can I still bill? What happens to the invoice numbers when it comes back?”
4. “How does an order reach my kitchen, and what happens if two orders come at the same second?”
5. “What does the GST invoice look like? Is my FSSAI number on it?”
6. “Which devices do I need to buy? Can I use the phones and tablets I already have?”
7. “What is the total cost in the first year, including setup, printers and any per-device fees?”
8. “If I leave, can I export my bills and customer data?”

A good vendor will be happy to show all eight in fifteen minutes.

## What a café POS really costs

Prices vary a lot, so compare the **first-year total**, not the headline number.

- **Software:** monthly or yearly subscriptions are common. Watch for separate fees per terminal, per outlet or per module (inventory, loyalty, online ordering).
- **Hardware:** a thermal bill printer is the main cost. 58 mm printers are cheap and compact; 80 mm printers give wider, easier-to-read bills. Tablets and phones you already own should be enough.
- **Setup and training:** some vendors charge for menu setup and training; others include it.
- **Hidden extras:** SMS packs, payment-gateway fees and annual maintenance contracts on bundled hardware.

## Red flags

- The demo uses a generic menu and the vendor avoids setting up yours.
- Billing stops completely when the internet does.
- You must buy their hardware to use the software.
- Getting your data out requires a support ticket.
- Discounts and voids are not logged with a reason, which makes cash leakage hard to spot.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best billing software for a small café in India?

The best billing software for a small café is one that prints correct GST invoices, handles UPI, cash and card on the same bill, sends orders to the kitchen reliably, keeps working when the internet drops and runs on devices you already own. Ask each vendor to demonstrate these with your own menu before you decide.

### Do I need a separate POS machine for my café?

Usually not. Many modern café POS systems run in a web browser on an Android tablet, an iPad, a laptop or even a phone. The one piece of hardware most cafés still buy is a thermal printer for bills and kitchen tickets.

### Can café POS software work without internet?

Some can and some cannot. Cloud-only systems stop billing when the connection drops. Offline-capable systems save bills on the device and sync them when the internet returns. Test this in the demo by switching off the Wi-Fi.

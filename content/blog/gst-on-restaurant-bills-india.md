GST on a café bill looks simple: 5%, split into CGST and SGST. But the details decide whether your bills hold up when your CA files returns, when a corporate guest asks for an invoice, or when a customer complains about a charge.

This guide covers what most café and restaurant owners need. It is general information, not tax advice. GST rules change, so confirm anything specific to your business with your CA.

## The GST rate on café and restaurant food

For most cafés and restaurants in India, food and drinks served in the restaurant, packed for takeaway or delivered are taxed at **5% without input tax credit (ITC)**. That means you charge 5% on the bill and cannot claim back the GST you paid on your purchases.

| Situation | Usual GST rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standalone café or restaurant (dine-in, takeaway, own delivery) | 5% | No input tax credit |
| Restaurant inside a hotel where any room is charged above ₹7,500 a day | 18% | Input tax credit allowed |
| Orders placed through food delivery apps | 5% | The delivery app collects and pays this GST, not the restaurant |
| Café on the composition scheme | Pays 5% of turnover | Cannot charge GST on the bill; issues a bill of supply |

For an ordinary café within a state, the 5% shows on the bill as **2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST**.

## Do you need GST registration?

A café supplies a service in GST terms. Registration is generally required once your annual turnover crosses **₹20 lakh** (₹10 lakh in special category states). Many cafés register earlier because corporate guests and landlords ask for a GSTIN. Your CA will also tell you whether delivery-app sales count towards your threshold in your case.

## Composition scheme or regular scheme?

The **composition scheme** is open to restaurants with turnover up to ₹1.5 crore (lower in some states). You pay 5% of your turnover as tax, file simpler returns and **must not charge GST to customers**. Your bills say “bill of supply” and show no tax line.

The **regular scheme** means you charge 5% on every bill, issue tax invoices and file regular returns. Choose with your CA. The point for your billing software: it must print the right kind of bill for your scheme.

## What a GST tax invoice must show

A café’s tax invoice should include:

- Your business name, address and **GSTIN**
- A **unique, consecutive invoice number** (up to 16 characters), with a fresh series for each financial year
- Date of the invoice
- Description, quantity and value of each item
- **SAC code** for restaurant services (996331 is commonly used for restaurant food services; confirm with your CA)
- Taxable value, the GST rate and the **CGST and SGST amounts shown separately**
- Total amount
- The customer’s name, address and GSTIN when the bill is for a registered business that asks for it
- Your **FSSAI licence or registration number**, which FSSAI requires on bills and cash receipts

## Service charge: what the rules say

Under guidelines from the Central Consumer Protection Authority, a restaurant **cannot add a service charge to the bill by default** or make it compulsory. If a guest chooses to pay one, it is voluntary. The Delhi High Court upheld these guidelines in 2025. If your current bills add service charge automatically, remove it.

## Common billing mistakes

1. **Gaps or repeats in invoice numbers.** This often happens when two devices bill at once or when a billing app restarts. Your software should keep one clean series per financial year.
2. **GST shown as one line** instead of CGST and SGST separately.
3. **Missing FSSAI number** on the bill.
4. **Discounts applied after tax.** Discounts given on the bill should reduce the taxable value before GST is calculated.
5. **Charging GST while on the composition scheme.** Composition cafés must not show GST on their bills.
6. **Deleting bills to “fix” mistakes.** Cancel or credit them properly instead, so the record stays complete.
7. **Paper bills at rush hour that never reach the books.** Every sale should be in the system, including those during an internet outage. See [billing when the internet goes down](/blog/restaurant-billing-software-offline/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the GST rate on restaurant food in India?

For most standalone cafés and restaurants, food and drinks are taxed at 5% without input tax credit, shown on the bill as 2.5% CGST and 2.5% SGST. Restaurants inside hotels where any room is charged above ₹7,500 a day are taxed at 18% with input tax credit.

### Can a café charge GST on the composition scheme?

No. A café on the composition scheme pays tax on its turnover but must not charge GST to customers. It issues a bill of supply instead of a tax invoice.

### Is service charge compulsory in restaurants in India?

No. Under the Central Consumer Protection Authority’s guidelines, upheld by the Delhi High Court in 2025, restaurants cannot add service charge by default. Guests may pay it voluntarily.

### Does a restaurant bill need an FSSAI number?

Yes. FSSAI requires food businesses to show their FSSAI licence or registration number on bills and cash receipts.

When someone nearby searches "café near me", Google shows them a map with three or four places. Which one they walk into depends heavily on two numbers: your star rating and how many reviews you have. A café with 4.5 stars from 600 reviews beats one with 4.7 stars from 40 almost every time.

The good part: most happy guests are willing to leave a review. They just aren't asked at the right moment, or it takes too many taps. Fix those two things and reviews start coming in on their own.

> **The short answer:** get your direct review link from Google Business Profile, then put it where guests already look after a good meal: the bill, the WhatsApp bill message and a small card on the table. Ask every guest the same way, never pay for reviews, and reply to every review within a couple of days.

## Why Google reviews matter so much for a café

- **They decide who shows up in the map results.** Google says local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence, and reviews are a big part of prominence.
- **Guests read them before they come.** Recent reviews tell people what to order, how busy it gets and whether it's good for a date or a laptop afternoon.
- **They tell you what to fix.** Three reviews mentioning slow bills is a clearer signal than any report.

## Step 1: Get your direct review link

Open your Google Business Profile (search your café's name on Google while signed in to the owner account) and look for **Ask for reviews** or **Get more reviews**. Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly, with the stars ready to tap.

Use this link everywhere. "Search for us on Google and find the reviews tab" loses most people. One tap keeps them.

If your profile is not verified yet, do that first: unverified profiles can't use all of these tools.

## Step 2: Ask at the right moment

The best moment is when the guest is happiest and still holding their phone:

- **Right after paying**, when the bill arrives on WhatsApp
- **When a dish lands that people photograph**, like a dessert or a latte with art
- **When a guest compliments the food or staff** out loud

The worst moments are while they're still waiting for food, or days later in a promotional message they won't open.

## Step 3: Put the link where guests already look

| Place | How |
|---|---|
| The WhatsApp bill | Add one line under the bill link: "Enjoyed your visit? A quick Google review helps us a lot 🙏" |
| The printed bill | A small QR code at the bottom: "Scan to review us on Google" |
| The table | A table card or tent card with the review QR, next to the menu QR |
| The counter | A small stand by the payment QR, where guests wait anyway |
| Instagram | The review link in your highlights or link in bio |

The WhatsApp bill is usually the most effective, because it is the one message every guest opens. Our guide to [sending restaurant bills on WhatsApp](/blog/send-restaurant-bill-on-whatsapp/) explains how to do that for free.

## Step 4: Make staff part of it, without pressure

Teach staff one simple, natural line, for example: "If you liked it, a Google review really helps us. The link is on your bill." That's all. Then:

- Mention staff by name in your replies when reviews praise them
- Share good reviews in the staff group so people see their effect
- Never make staff chase guests or hover while they write

## Step 5: Reply to every review

Replies are public, and future guests read them as closely as the reviews.

**For a good review:** thank them by name, mention something specific they liked and invite them back. Keep it to two or three lines.

**For a bad review:** stay calm, apologise for the experience, say what you have changed and offer to make it right offline. Don't argue or share private details. A thoughtful reply to a 2-star review often wins more trust than ten 5-star reviews.

Try to reply within a day or two. Google lets you reply from the Business Profile on your phone.

## What Google does not allow

Breaking these rules can get reviews removed or your profile restricted:

- **No paying for reviews.** That includes discounts, free desserts or freebies in exchange for a review.
- **No asking only happy guests.** Google calls this "review gating": asking everyone is fine, filtering who gets asked is not.
- **No fake reviews.** Not from staff, family, friends or paid services.
- **No reviews written on the premises by staff on behalf of guests.**

Asking every guest the same way and making it easy is completely allowed, and it works.

## Step 6: Use what reviews tell you

Once a month, read your last 30 reviews and note which words come up again and again. "Slow", "bill", "waited" and "wrong order" point at operations, not marketing. Fixing them raises your rating faster than any campaign. ## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get my Google review link for my restaurant?

Open your Google Business Profile while signed in as the owner and choose "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews". Google gives you a short link that opens the review box directly. Share that link or turn it into a QR code.

### Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Google's policy does not allow incentives for reviews, including discounts, free items or giveaways. You can ask every guest to leave a review, but you can't reward them for it.

### How many Google reviews does a café need?

There is no fixed number, but more recent reviews help both ranking and trust. Aim for a steady flow every week rather than a burst once a year, since guests and Google both value recent reviews.

### Should I reply to negative reviews?

Yes, politely and quickly. Apologise, explain what you've changed and offer to talk offline. Future guests judge how you handle problems as much as the problem itself.

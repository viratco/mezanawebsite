Ads bring someone in once. Regulars keep a café alive. A guest who visits every week and spends ₹350 is worth more than ₹18,000 a year to you, and they bring friends.

Most regulars are not won by discounts. They are won by being remembered. Here is how small cafés do it on purpose.

## 1. Make the first visit easy to repeat

Guests come back to places that feel consistent. The cold coffee should taste the same on Tuesday as it did on Saturday, and the wait should be predictable. Before spending a rupee on marketing, fix the things that make first-timers hesitate: slow ordering at rush hour, wrong orders and long waits for the bill. ## 2. Remember names and “the usual”

Nothing builds loyalty like “The usual, Sharma ji?” Your best staff do this naturally, but they forget and they move on. Write it down in your system instead: name, favourite order, how often they come. Then anyone on shift can greet a regular properly.

## 3. Collect numbers the right way: with consent

A list of guests you can message is the most valuable thing a small café can own. But collect it properly:

- **Ask, don’t take.** Let guests choose to share their name and number, for example as an optional field when they order from the table QR.
- **Say what it is for.** “Can we send you offers now and then?” is clear.
- **Keep a separate yes for offers.** Sharing a number for the order is not the same as agreeing to promotions.
- **Make it easy to stop.** If someone asks not to be messaged, remove them.

India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act expects businesses to collect personal data with clear consent and for a stated purpose, so this is the right habit to build now.

## 4. Message like a person, not a brand

WhatsApp works for cafés because it feels personal. Keep it that way:

- **Use their name** and mention their favourite: “Hi Nikhil, it’s been a while. Your cappuccino is 20% off this weekend.”
- **Message rarely.** Once or twice a month is plenty. More than that and you are spam.
- **Time it well.** Slow weekday afternoons and rainy days are when an offer fills empty tables.
- **Target the right people.** Guests who haven’t visited in three weeks need a nudge. Guests who came yesterday don’t.

## 5. Reward regulars, not everyone

Blanket discounts teach every guest to wait for a discount. Small, personal rewards work better: a free cookie on the tenth visit, a birthday dessert, or the first taste of a new drink for your top guests. Keep it simple enough that staff can run it without a manual.

## 6. Answer every review

Reply to Google reviews, the good and the bad. Thank people by name, fix what they complained about and say so. Future guests read your replies as much as the reviews.

## 7. Track who comes back

You can’t improve what you don’t count. Every month, look at:

- How many guests visited more than once
- Who your top 20 guests are by visits and by spend
- Which regulars haven’t come back in a while

Start with your own baseline and aim to improve it month on month.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I collect customer phone numbers legally at my café?

Ask guests to share their number voluntarily, explain what you will use it for and keep a separate opt-in for promotional messages. Remove anyone who asks to stop. This follows the consent-first approach of India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act.

### How often should a café send WhatsApp offers to customers?

Once or twice a month is enough for most cafés. Personal, well-timed messages, such as an offer on a slow weekday for a guest who hasn’t visited in a few weeks, work far better than frequent broadcasts.

### What is the cheapest way to get repeat customers?

Remember your regulars. Recording names and usual orders, greeting guests personally and sending occasional, relevant offers to people who opted in costs almost nothing and outperforms most paid ads for a local café.

Most Indian cafés already have a QR code on the table. Usually it opens a PDF of the menu, and the guest still has to wave at a waiter to order. **QR ordering** goes one step further: the guest scans, picks dishes on their own phone and sends the order straight to your counter and kitchen.

Done well, it is the single biggest relief at rush hour. Done badly, guests hate it and staff quietly stop using it. This guide covers both.

## Three kinds of table QR codes

| Type | What the guest does | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| QR menu | Scans and reads the menu (often a PDF or image) | Printing costs, menu updates |
| QR ordering | Scans, chooses dishes and options, places the order | Waiting for a waiter, order-taking mistakes, rush hour |
| QR ordering and payment | Orders and pays from the phone | Waiting for the bill |

Only the second and third change how your café runs. If your current QR opens a PDF, you have a digital menu, not an ordering system.

## How a QR order flows through a café

1. **Scan.** Each table has its own QR card, so the system knows the order is from Table 6. No app download, just the phone camera.
2. **Choose.** The guest sees dishes with photos, sizes and add-ons, filters to veg only if they want, and adds items to a cart.
3. **Send.** The order arrives at the counter screen instantly.
4. **Accept.** Staff accept the order with one tap. This step matters: it stops prank orders and lets you catch anything unusual.
5. **Kitchen.** Accepted orders appear on the kitchen screen or print as a [kitchen order ticket](/blog/what-is-kot-kitchen-order-ticket/).
6. **Bill.** Everything the table ordered collects on one running bill, ready to settle by UPI, cash or card.

## Why owners switch it on

- **Rush hour stops depending on one waiter.** Ten tables can order at the same time.
- **Fewer mistakes.** The guest chose “oat milk, less sugar” themselves; nobody had to remember it.
- **Higher order value.** Photos and add-ons sell. Guests who browse at their own pace often add a dessert or a second drink.
- **Staff time goes to service.** Instead of writing orders, staff serve food, clear tables and talk to guests.
- **You learn who your guests are.** If the order screen offers an optional name and phone number, you can build a list of regulars. See [how to get repeat customers](/blog/how-to-get-repeat-customers-cafe/).

## The worries, honestly

**“My older guests won’t use it.”** Some won’t, and they shouldn’t have to. Keep waiter ordering available. The best setups let staff add orders to the same table bill from their own phone.

**“It feels impersonal.”** Only if staff disappear. Use the time QR ordering saves to check on tables.

**“What if someone orders from home for a joke?”** That is what the accept step is for. Orders only reach the kitchen once staff accept them.

**“Can guests change prices?”** In a properly built system, no. Prices are always taken from your menu on the server, never from what the guest’s phone sends.

## Setting it up well

- **One QR card per table,** printed clearly and placed where it can be seen. Number every table.
- **Photos for your top 15 dishes.** You don’t need all of them on day one.
- **Sizes and add-ons set up properly.** “Extra shot” as an add-on beats a note field every time.
- **Test from a guest’s phone** on mobile data, not your café Wi-Fi.
- **Tell guests.** A small line on the card helps: “Scan to order, no app needed.”

## Mistakes that make cafés give up

- **Forcing an app download.** Most guests leave. QR ordering should open in the phone’s browser.
- **A slow PDF menu instead of a real ordering page.** Guests zoom, give up and wave for a waiter anyway.
- **No accept step,** so the kitchen gets orders that nobody checked.
- **Orders that don’t reach the kitchen automatically,** so staff re-type them. That doubles the work.
- **QR only.** Always keep waiter ordering for guests who prefer it.

## Is QR ordering right for your café?

It suits table-service cafés and restaurants with regular rushes, young or office crowds, and menus with customisations. It matters less for a counter-only tea stall or a café that is mostly takeaway.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do guests need to download an app to order by QR code?

No. A good QR ordering system opens in the phone’s web browser straight from the camera. Asking guests to install an app is the fastest way to make them stop using it.

### Is QR code ordering good for older customers?

Many older guests are comfortable with it, but some are not. Keep waiter ordering available and let staff add orders to the same table bill, so every guest can choose.

### How much does QR ordering cost for a café?

It is usually included in a café POS subscription rather than sold separately. Apart from the software, the only cost is printing a QR card for each table.

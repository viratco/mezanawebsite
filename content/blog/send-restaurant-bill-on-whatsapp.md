Guests lose paper bills. They ask for one again when they need to claim expenses, check a charge, or forward it to the friend who owes them half. A bill on WhatsApp ends that problem: it sits in their chat, it can't fade, and it carries your café's name every time they scroll past it.

The good news is that sending a bill on WhatsApp doesn't have to cost you anything. Here is how it works, which method to choose, and what to write.

> **The short answer:** after the guest pays, open WhatsApp with their number and a ready-made message containing a link to their tax invoice and your Google review link. Send it from your café's own WhatsApp. It costs ₹0 per bill. Paid WhatsApp API tools only make sense once you want fully automatic, bulk messaging.

## Why send bills on WhatsApp at all?

- **Guests actually keep them.** Almost every guest in India has WhatsApp open all day. A bill there is easier to find than a thermal slip in a wallet.
- **Fewer "can you resend my bill?" calls.** Corporate guests and people splitting the bill can forward it themselves.
- **Your number gets saved.** A message from your café makes it easy for the guest to save you and come back to order or book a table.
- **A natural moment to ask for a review.** The bill message is the one message every guest opens, right after the meal. Adding your Google review link there brings in reviews without extra effort. (More on that in our guide to [getting more Google reviews](/blog/how-to-get-more-google-reviews-restaurant/).)
- **Less paper.** You can still print when a guest wants a paper copy, but you don't have to print every bill.

## The three ways to send a bill on WhatsApp

| Method | Cost per bill | Effort | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo of the printed bill | ₹0 | High: print, photograph, find contact, attach | Occasional requests |
| Link from your POS, sent from your own WhatsApp | ₹0 | One tap and Send | Most cafés and restaurants |
| WhatsApp Business API (automatic) | Charged per message | None once set up, but setup and approval take time | Large chains sending thousands of messages |

### 1. A photo of the printed bill

It works, but it is slow at a busy counter, the photo is often blurry, and you still waste the paper. It also means saving every guest's number on the counter phone manually.

### 2. A bill link sent from your own WhatsApp (free)

Your billing software creates a private web link for each paid bill: the guest taps it and sees their full tax invoice. The software opens WhatsApp with the guest's number and the message already typed. Your staff only tap Send.

Because the message goes from your café's own WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business app, there is no per-message charge. This is the method we recommend for almost every café and small restaurant.

### 3. The WhatsApp Business API (paid)

The API lets software send messages automatically, with no one tapping Send. Meta charges for this per message, and providers usually add their own fee on top. At the time of writing (November 2026), a utility message such as a bill costs roughly ₹0.12 per message in India before GST, and promotional messages cost several times more. You also need a verified business, approved message templates and a provider account.

That is worth it for a chain sending thousands of bills a day. For a 10 to 30 table café, it adds cost and setup for something one tap already does.

## What to put in the bill message

Keep it short, warm and useful. A good bill message has four parts:

1. **A thank-you with the guest's name and your café's name.**
2. **The total**, so they can see it without opening the link.
3. **The link to the tax invoice.**
4. **Your Google review link**, as a gentle ask, not a demand.

For example:

> Hi Riya! Thank you for visiting **Brew & Bloom** ☕
>
> Your bill (₹714):
> app.mezana.in/b/…
>
> Enjoyed your visit? A quick Google review helps us a lot 🙏

Avoid long paragraphs, offers stuffed into the bill, or five emojis per line. The bill is a service message. Keep promotions for separate, occasional messages to guests who have agreed to receive them.

## What the online bill should show

The link should open a proper tax invoice, the same as your printed one:

- Café name, address, GSTIN and FSSAI number
- Invoice number and date
- Each item with quantity and price
- Discount, if any, with the amount
- Taxable value, CGST and SGST shown separately, and the total
- How it was paid: UPI, cash, card or a mix

If you're unsure what a correct restaurant invoice needs, read our guide to [GST on restaurant bills in India](/blog/gst-on-restaurant-bills-india/).

The link should also be **private**: a long, random link for each bill, not a simple number anyone could guess. Nobody should be able to change the number in the address and see another guest's bill.

## Consent and good manners

Sending a guest the bill they just paid for, because they gave you their number, is a normal service message. A few rules keep it that way:

- **Ask before saving a number.** "Shall I send the bill on WhatsApp?" is enough at the counter.
- **Keep bills and offers separate.** Only send promotional messages to guests who have opted in to them.
- **Stop when asked.** If a guest says they don't want messages, remove them.

This follows the consent-first approach of India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and keeps your WhatsApp number from being reported as spam.

## Common mistakes

- **Sending from a staff member's personal number.** Use one café phone with WhatsApp Business so the chat history stays with the café, not with whoever was on shift.
- **Typing the bill by hand.** Typos in amounts cause disputes. Let the software write the message.
- **Forgetting the review link.** It is the cheapest marketing you will ever do.
- **Paying for an API you don't need.** Most cafés never send enough bills to make it worth it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I send a restaurant bill on WhatsApp for free?

Yes. If your billing software creates a link for each bill and opens WhatsApp with the message typed, you send it from your café's own WhatsApp at no cost. Only the WhatsApp Business API, which sends messages automatically, is charged per message.

### Is a bill sent on WhatsApp a valid GST invoice?

The online bill should show the same details as a printed tax invoice: your GSTIN, invoice number and date, items, taxable value, CGST and SGST, and the total. If it does, sending it digitally is just a different way of handing it over. Ask your CA if you have specific compliance questions.

### Do I need the WhatsApp Business API to send bills?

No. The API is only needed if you want messages to go out automatically without anyone tapping Send, usually at chain scale. A free WhatsApp or WhatsApp Business app on the café phone is enough for sending bills one by one.

### Should I put offers in the bill message?

No. Keep the bill message short: thank-you, total, bill link and review link. Send offers separately, and only to guests who agreed to receive them.

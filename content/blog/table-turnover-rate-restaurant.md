On a busy evening, your café's capacity isn't the number of chairs. It's how many times each table gets used. A 12-table café that turns each table three times serves 36 groups; the same café turning tables twice serves 24. Same rent, same staff, a third less business.

The good news is that most of the time a table is "taken" isn't spent eating. It's spent waiting: to order, for the food, and especially for the bill. Cut the waiting and you serve more guests while they feel *less* rushed.

> **The short answer:** table turnover rate = number of groups served ÷ number of tables, for one service period. To improve it, shorten the waits around the meal (ordering, the kitchen ticket reaching the cook, and the bill), not the meal itself.

## How to calculate table turnover rate

**Table turnover rate = Number of groups (bills) served ÷ Number of tables**

Measure it for one service period, like lunch or dinner, not a whole day, because the two behave very differently.

**Example:** a café has 12 tables. Between 7 pm and 11 pm it closes 30 dine-in bills.

30 ÷ 12 = **2.5 turns** during dinner.

You can also look at the **average time a table is occupied**: from when guests sit down to when they leave. If your 4-hour dinner service gives 2.5 turns, each table is occupied for about 96 minutes on average, including the gaps between groups.

## What a good turnover rate looks like

It depends on the kind of place:

| Type of place | What's typical |
|---|---|
| Quick-service café, coffee and snacks | Tables turn fast, often several times an hour at peak |
| Casual café with full meals | Often 2 to 3 turns per meal period |
| Casual dining restaurant | Often 1.5 to 2.5 turns per meal period |
| Fine dining | Often 1 to 1.5 turns, by design |

A laptop-friendly café that wants people to stay is making a different choice from a busy lunch spot, and that's fine. What matters is that **when there's a queue at the door**, tables aren't sitting idle while guests wait for things they've already asked for.

## Where the time actually goes

Watch a few tables on a busy night with a stopwatch, and you'll usually see something like this:

1. **Waiting for a menu or a waiter** to take the order
2. **Waiting while the order is written down**, carried to the kitchen and read
3. **Eating** (the only part guests came for)
4. **Waiting for the bill** after asking for it
5. **Waiting for payment**: finding change, the card machine or a UPI QR
6. **The table sitting empty** until someone clears and resets it

Steps 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 can easily add 20 to 30 minutes to a visit on a busy night. None of it makes guests happier.

## Seven ways to turn tables faster (without rushing anyone)

1. **Let guests order the moment they sit down.** With [QR table ordering](/qr-code-ordering-system/), guests can browse and order from their own phone straight away, instead of waiting for a free waiter.
2. **Get orders to the kitchen instantly.** A [kitchen order screen](/kot-software/) that rings for every order removes the walk to the pass and the lost slips that make dishes late.
3. **Bring the bill in seconds, not minutes.** "Bill please" followed by a 10-minute wait is the most common complaint in café reviews. A POS that makes the GST bill in one tap fixes it.
4. **Make paying easy.** Keep a UPI QR at every table or the counter, and let one bill be split across UPI, cash and card without arithmetic.
5. **Clear and reset tables right away.** Assign who resets which section, and keep a small station of clean cutlery and menus near the floor.
6. **Seat groups at the right size table.** Two people at a six-seater block four seats. Keep a few two-tops for peak hours.
7. **Know your peaks.** If Tuesday 8 to 9 pm is your busiest slot, that's when you need your fastest staff on the floor, not on a break.

## What one extra turn is worth

Say your average bill is ₹800. If faster ordering and billing let 4 of your tables seat one extra group each on a busy evening:

4 extra groups × ₹800 = **₹3,200 more that evening.**

Over 20 busy evenings in a month, that's about **₹64,000** in sales, from the same tables, staff and rent. Your numbers will differ, but the idea holds: the cheapest new seats are the ones you already have.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is table turnover rate?

It's how many times, on average, each table is used by a new group during a service period. Divide the number of groups (or bills) served by the number of tables.

### How can I increase table turnover without rushing guests?

Cut the waiting around the meal: let guests order as soon as they sit, get orders to the kitchen instantly, bring the bill quickly and make paying easy. Guests feel better served while tables free up sooner.

### What is a good table turnover rate for a café?

It depends on your concept. Casual cafés serving full meals often see 2 to 3 turns per meal period, while quick coffee-and-snack cafés turn much faster. Compare your own busy periods week to week.

### Does QR ordering really make tables turn faster?

It removes the wait for a waiter to take the order and the time spent writing and carrying orders to the kitchen. On busy nights that's often several minutes per table, plus fewer wrong orders that need remaking.

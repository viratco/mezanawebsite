Every café has lived this Saturday: a slip falls behind the coffee machine, Table 7 waits twenty minutes for a sandwich nobody started, and the guest leaves unhappy. The fix starts with understanding the humble **KOT**.

## What is a KOT?

A **KOT (Kitchen Order Ticket)** is the instruction that tells the kitchen what to cook. It is created when an order is taken and goes to the kitchen, not to the guest. A KOT usually shows:

- The table number (or “takeaway”)
- Each dish with its quantity
- Options and notes, like “less spicy” or “oat milk”
- The time the order was placed
- A KOT number

The **bill** is a different document. The bill is what the guest pays. In a well-run café, every item on a bill traces back to a KOT, and every KOT ends up on a bill.

## Why paper KOTs fail at rush hour

Handwritten or printed slips work when the café is quiet. At rush hour they break down:

- **Slips get lost** under plates, behind machines or in a pocket.
- **Handwriting is misread.** “1 masala” becomes “7 masala”.
- **Order is unclear.** The kitchen cooks the slip on top, not the oldest one.
- **Nobody knows how long a table has waited.**
- **Changes get missed.** “Cancel the fries” never reaches the person frying.
- **The front doesn’t know when food is ready,** so dishes sit and cool on the pass.

## What a kitchen display screen (KDS) does

A **kitchen display screen** replaces paper slips with a tablet or screen in the kitchen. When an order is accepted at the counter, it appears on the kitchen screen instantly.

A good kitchen screen:

- **Rings** on every new order, so nobody has to keep looking at it.
- **Shows big, readable type** from the stove: table, quantity, options and notes.
- **Shows how long each order has waited,** oldest first.
- **Lets the cook mark a dish ready,** with a confirmation so a splash of water can’t send something out by mistake.
- **Tells the front staff the moment food is ready,** so it reaches the table hot.
- **Updates live** when an order changes or is cancelled.

## KOT numbers and cash leakage

KOTs also protect your money. If every dish cooked has a KOT and every KOT must be billed, it becomes very hard for an order to be served and never billed. When you check your day, compare KOTs with bills. Anything cooked but not billed is a question worth asking.

Discounts and removed items deserve the same care. A good system keeps removed dishes visible (struck through) and records the reason for each discount, so nothing disappears quietly.

## Paper, screen or both?

| Setup | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Handwritten slips | Very small cafés with one cook | Lost slips, handwriting, no timing |
| Printed KOTs from the POS | Kitchens without space for a screen | Paper still gets lost; changes need a reprint |
| Kitchen screen | Most cafés with a rush | Needs a tablet mounted where the cook can see it |
| Screen plus printer | Busy kitchens with several stations | Slightly more setup |

Many cafés start with a kitchen screen on an old tablet and keep a 58 mm printer for reprints. Some also send the kitchen ticket to a WhatsApp group as a backup.

## Setting up a kitchen screen

1. **Mount the tablet** at eye level, away from steam and splashes.
2. **Turn the volume up** and keep it plugged in.
3. **Use big text** and keep only active orders on screen.
4. **Agree on one rule:** a dish is “ready” only when it is on the pass.
5. **Run a test lunch** with staff ordering from their phones before you go live.

If you are also adding [QR table ordering](/blog/qr-code-ordering-for-restaurants/), orders from guests’ phones should flow to the same kitchen screen after the counter accepts them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the full form of KOT in a restaurant?

KOT stands for Kitchen Order Ticket. It is the ticket that tells the kitchen what to prepare for a table or takeaway order.

### What is the difference between a KOT and a bill?

A KOT goes to the kitchen and tells cooks what to make. A bill goes to the guest and shows what they owe, including GST. In a well-run café, every item on a bill matches a KOT.

### Do I need a KOT printer?

Not necessarily. A kitchen display screen on a tablet can replace printed KOTs. Many cafés still keep a small thermal printer for reprints or as a backup.
